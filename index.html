03 / Method

From report to recorded decision.

The process is deliberately short. Scope and impact are settled before money is discussed, and every state change leaves a record.

  1. 01

    Scope

    Check the program’s assets, impacts, and exclusions. If a target is ambiguous, ask before testing.

  2. 02

    Report

    Send a summary with reproduction steps, affected addresses, and the impact you believe you can demonstrate.

  3. 03

    Triage

    We reproduce the issue and assign severity from impact and exploitability. You receive the reasoning, not just a label.

  4. 04

    Decision

    Accepted findings move to payout. Rejected findings receive a written reason. Both decisions are appended to the evidence chain.

  5. 05

    Payment

    Payout is made in PCENT under the program terms. The payout transaction remains linked to the submission record.

Severity is set on reproduced impact

ClassTypical condition
CriticalDirect loss or permanent lock of funds; protocol insolvency; unauthenticated privilege escalation.
HighConditional loss of funds; bypass of a core invariant; privileged action without authorization.
MediumBounded impact, griefing, or denial of service with a practical recovery path.
LowInformation exposure, hardening gaps, or issues requiring unrealistic preconditions.

A theoretical issue without a path to impact is usually informational.

04 / Evidence

The integrity chain.

A decision is only useful if you can check it later. Each submission carries its own chain of state transitions, hashed end to end.

A finding is not a static row in a database. Each status change is a link in a SHA-256 hash chain. The link contains the submission ID, transition, reviewer, a hash of the reviewer’s IP, a timestamp, a hash of the justification, and the previous link’s hash. Change any field and the link’s self-hash changes. Break the chain and the submission is invalidated.

  • Canonical hashing: JSON with sorted keys, SHA-256.
  • Allowed transitions: pending → reviewing → paid or rejected.
  • Reviewer IPs appear only as SHA-256 hashes.
  • Any change to a link breaks its self-hash and invalidates the record.

What the chain proves. That the record was not edited after the decision. It does not prove the finding itself is correct; review and reproduction do that.

08 / Questions

Questions researchers ask.

If the answer is not here, send the question with your report. It is read by the lab.

Who can submit a finding?

Anyone. You do not need a prior relationship with TKO Labs. Send a clear summary with reproduction steps and the program you are reporting under.

What is in scope?

Each program card names its target surface. The core program covers the web application, API, authentication, and deployment configuration. The token and vault programs cover their contracts. Testing must not disrupt live service or third-party systems.

Do I need PCENT to submit?

You need the review activation fee published on the program: 1,000 PCENT, or 500 PCENT for JRTS. Staking is optional. It changes queue priority and payout terms but is not required to file a report.

How are payouts made?

Accepted findings are paid in PCENT under the program terms. The platform records the payout transaction against the submission, and the decision remains in the evidence chain.

Are these mainnet contracts?

No. The addresses published here are Sepolia testnet deployments. Testnet assets have no market value. Do not send mainnet funds.

How should I send a sensitive report?

Send a summary first and ask for a secure channel. We will confirm scope and the channel before you share exploit details. Never send private keys, seed phrases, or third-party personal data.

What if my report is rejected?

You receive a written reason. The rejection is appended to the evidence chain. If you have new reproduction evidence, reply to the thread and we will re-open triage.

Do you take private audits?

Yes. Private audits are scoped and quoted separately. Start with a short email describing the codebase, timeline, and what you need reviewed.
